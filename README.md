# Embodied Glucose Feedback

## Research question
**Does the way we perceive physiological data become part of the physiology it describes?**

**Can different representations of identical CGM information produce measurably different neural and autonomic responses?**

**Primary measures:** EEG + EDA + HRV

This project investigates whether different sensory representations of continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) data alter neural, autonomic, behavioral, and ultimately metabolic responses.

## Concept
CGM creates a new sensory channel through which people continuously perceive their internal metabolic state. From the perspective of **predictive processing and interoception**, this feedback may become incorporated into the brain’s model of the body rather than functioning as neutral information.

We hypothesize that **less judgmental and less numerically explicit representations of glucose may preserve useful awareness while reducing unnecessary neural and autonomic reactivity to glucose fluctuations.**

The proposed feedback loop is:

**glucose state → sensory representation → brain response → autonomic response → behavior → glucose dynamics**

## Novelty
Most CGM research focuses on accuracy, usability, or clinical outcomes. This project instead treats the **representation of physiological information itself as an experimental variable**.

It asks whether identical metabolic information presented numerically, graphically, abstractly, or haptically produces different physiological responses.

## Methods
Participants using CGM would receive standardized representations of the same glucose information, for example:

- numerical value and trend arrow
- conventional glucose graph
- abstract visual feedback
- haptic feedback
- no-feedback control

Acute responses would be measured using **EEG**, **EDA**, **HRV**, and potentially pupil dilation or respiration, alongside CGM trajectories and behavioral measures such as perceived urgency, checking behavior, and correction intent.

## Feasibility
The project can begin with a compact experimental study using existing CGM systems, wearable physiological sensors, and relatively simple custom feedback interfaces. The initial aim is not to demonstrate long-term glycemic improvement, but to establish whether **representation alone produces measurable differences in neural and autonomic response**.

## Potential impact
The work could establish a new way of thinking about health interfaces: not simply as displays of physiology, but as components within a **closed human physiological feedback loop**.

Beyond diabetes, the concept could extend to heart rate, HRV, blood pressure, sleep, stress, SpO₂, pain, and other continuously monitored health metrics.
