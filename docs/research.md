# Research Publications

This section collects peer-reviewed work relevant to the core project question:

> **Does the way we perceive physiological data become part of the physiology it describes?**

The literature below is organized around the proposed pathway:

**physiological state → representation/perception → neural response → autonomic response → behavior → physiological change**

## 1. Personalized physiological feedback and brain activity

### Whelan et al. (2017) — *Brain Activation in Response to Personalized Behavioral and Physiological Feedback From Self-Monitoring Technology: Pilot Study*
**Journal:** Journal of Medical Internet Research  
**PMID:** 29117928  
**Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29117928/

A particularly direct precedent for this project. Participants wore activity and glucose sensors for 14 days, then viewed personalized feedback during fMRI. Personalized glucose feedback produced significantly greater prefrontal activation than behavioral feedback.

**Relevance:** Demonstrates that personalized glucose information can evoke a measurable neural response and supports treating health-data feedback itself as an experimental stimulus.

## 2. Interoception, predictive processing, and physiological feedback

### Khalsa et al. (2018) — *Interoception and Mental Health: A Roadmap*
**Journal:** Biological Psychiatry: Cognitive Neuroscience and Neuroimaging  
**Context:** Foundational framework for studying interoception across sensing, interpretation, and regulation.

**Relevance:** Provides conceptual grounding for glucose data as an external representation of an internal bodily state.

### Petzschner et al. (2018) — *Interoceptive inference: From computational neuroscience to clinic*
**Journal:** Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews  
**PMID:** 29694845  
**Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29694845/

Frames interoception using predictive coding and active inference, connecting central and autonomic nervous-system regulation with homeostasis.

**Relevance:** Strong theoretical basis for asking whether externally presented CGM information modifies the brain's predictive model of metabolic state.

### Khalsa et al. (2021) — *The Emerging Science of Interoception: Sensing, Integrating, Interpreting, and Regulating Signals within the Self*
**Journal:** Trends in Neurosciences  
**PMID:** 33378655  
**Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33378655/

Presents a unified framework for sensing, interpreting, integrating, and regulating internal physiological signals.

**Relevance:** Useful framework for separating stages of the proposed feedback loop and defining measurable outcomes.

### Critchley & Garfinkel (2018) — *The neurobiology of interoception in health and disease*
**Journal:** Current Opinion in Psychology  
**PMID:** 29974959  
**Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29974959/

Reviews neural and autonomic mechanisms of interoception and their relation to homeostatic and allostatic regulation.

**Relevance:** Supports the hypothesis that perception of bodily state can engage systems that also regulate physiology.

### Interoceptive technologies for psychiatric interventions (2023)
**Journal:** Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews  
**PMID:** 38007168  
**Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38007168/

Reviews technologies for experimentally manipulating interoception, including artificial sensations, false feedback, conditioning, and multimodal interventions.

**Relevance:** Especially useful methodologically for designing controlled visual, haptic, or altered-feedback paradigms.

## 3. Stress, autonomic response, and glucose dynamics

### Wiesli et al. (2005) — *Acute psychological stress affects glucose concentrations in patients with type 1 diabetes following food intake but not in the fasting state*
**Journal:** Diabetes Care  
**PMID:** 16043731  
**DOI:** 10.2337/diacare.28.8.1910  
**Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16043731/

Using the Trier Social Stress Test, acute psychosocial stress significantly delayed the postprandial decline in glucose in people with type 1 diabetes. Blood pressure and heart rate also rose substantially.

**Relevance:** Provides direct evidence that a psychologically induced state can alter subsequent glucose dynamics in T1D, supporting the plausibility of the pathway **perception → autonomic/endocrine response → glucose**.

### Outpatient Randomized Crossover Automated Insulin Delivery Versus Conventional Therapy with Induced Stress Challenges (2022)
**PMID:** 35049354  
**Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35049354/

Adults with T1D underwent laboratory psychological and pharmacological stress challenges while glucose, insulin, heart rate, and cortisol responses were monitored.

**Relevance:** Demonstrates a feasible experimental structure for simultaneously studying induced psychological state, autonomic/endocrine markers, and CGM-derived glucose outcomes.

## 4. CGM presentation, alerts, and behavior

### Everett et al. (2026) — *The Emotional Burden and Behavioral Impacts of Alarms and Alerts Across the Spectrum of Diabetes Technology Use: a Cross-Sectional Study*
**Journal:** JMIR Diabetes  
**PMID:** 42594259  
**Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42594259/

Reports substantial alarm burden and frequent behavioral overcorrection following high- and low-glucose alerts.

**Relevance:** Evidence that the way glucose information is delivered can trigger behavioral responses that may themselves affect glycemic outcomes.

---

## 5. Biofeedback and measurable interoceptive change

### *The Utility of Heartrate and Heartrate Variability Biofeedback for the Improvement of Interoception across Behavioural, Physiological and Neural Outcome Measures: A Systematic Review* (2024)
**PMID:** 38928579  
**Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38928579/

Systematic review of 77 studies examining HR/HRV biofeedback across behavioral, physiological, and neural measures of interoception.

**Relevance:** Shows that feedback about internal physiology can be studied simultaneously at behavioral, autonomic, and neural levels, closely paralleling the proposed CGM paradigm.

## Emerging research gap

Taken together, the literature supports several individual links:

- physiological feedback can produce measurable brain activation;
- interoceptive perception is closely linked with autonomic regulation;
- psychological state can alter glucose dynamics;
- CGM alerts can alter user behavior;
- physiological feedback can be manipulated experimentally across sensory modalities.

What appears much less studied is the **full closed loop**:

> **Does changing the sensory representation of the same glucose state change neural and autonomic activity enough to alter behavior and/or subsequent glucose dynamics?**

This is the central gap addressed by **Embodied Glucose Feedback**.

## Candidate experimental outcomes

The literature suggests a practical multimodal measurement set:

- EEG / event-related potentials
- ECG and HRV
- electrodermal activity
- respiration
- pupil dilation
- salivary cortisol, where feasible
- CGM trajectory and rate-of-change
- perceived urgency / stress
- correction intent
- checking behavior
